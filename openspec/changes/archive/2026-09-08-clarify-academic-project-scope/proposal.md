## Why

Public-facing text currently describes standard AFL++ outcomes without consistently stating the project's academic, offline, and authorization boundaries. Short excerpts can therefore misrepresent a software-quality research prototype as an operational tool.

## What Changes

- State the academic robustness-testing purpose and authorized, isolated-laboratory boundary prominently in the README.
- Use neutral, precise terms such as abnormal termination, anomalous output, candidate sample, and automated input generation in active documentation and runtime messages.
- Explain that retained AFL++ names and quoted publication titles are third-party technical terms, not statements of project intent.
- Add a terminology and responsible-use section for contributors.
- Preserve historical research records while labeling their archival context.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `academic-project-communication`: Defines scope statements and terminology for public project materials.

### Modified Capabilities

None.

## Impact

The README, active project documentation, selected runtime messages/comments, contributor guidance, and banners on archived research notes are affected. Program behavior and third-party APIs are unchanged.
